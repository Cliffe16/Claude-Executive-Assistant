# 03 – Discovery & site visits: diagnose before proposing

The first meeting should leave you knowing what is broken, what "good" looks like for this buyer, and what operations would have to deliver. Treat a 15–20-minute visit as a structured conversation, not a tour of our brochure.

## A practical 15-minute visit

| Minutes | Action | Exact prompt or check |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 | Establish purpose | "What made waste management worth discussing now?" |
| 2–5 | Map current process | "Walk me through collection day from the time waste is placed out until the area is clean." |
| 5–8 | Quantify friction | "How often has a collection been late in the past month? Who handles complaints?" |
| 8–11 | Observe operations | Look at bags, bins, vehicle route, gate hours, water ingress, hazardous/segregated waste. |
| 11–13 | Map buying process | "Who signs off, what is the notice period and which documents will procurement need?" |
| 13–15 | Agree next step | Recap need, confirm what you will send and book a decision conversation. |

## The seven facts without which a quote is guesswork

1. **Site location and access:** exact gate, loading area, parking, opening hours and whether trucks can enter.
2. **Waste stream:** household, commercial, food, construction, sharps, chemical or another category; unknowns must be flagged.
3. **Volume:** number of occupied units, bags/bins per day, peak days, expected changes after occupancy.
4. **Frequency and response:** days, time window, contingency process and extra-trip expectations.
5. **Decision path:** budget owner, approving authority, tender/notice period, start-date dependency.
6. **Commercial details:** entity being invoiced, VAT treatment, eTIMS needs, deposit and payment term.
7. **Proof of performance:** collection log, photographs where appropriate, weights, monthly summary or disposal evidence.

## Listen for the cost of inaction

- If they say "The current supplier is fine," ask: **"What would you change about the service if you had the choice?"**
- If they say "Residents keep complaining," ask: **"How often does this reach the manager, and what happens when it does?"**

The second answer reveals the economic and emotional driver.
