# 02A – Buying committee & incentives

A visit can go brilliantly and still create no sale if the person who liked you cannot approve the contract. Leave with two facts: **who controls the decision** and **why that person would find a meeting worthwhile**. An introduction request is a sales step, not an awkward afterthought.

## Buying map (draw it before sending a quotation)

| Role | What they know or control | How to engage |
|---|---|---|
| Daily user / site contact | Caretaker, cleaner or site engineer knows waste volume, complaints and gate access. | Listen, verify the real problem and ask for the manager by role and name. Do not mistake operational consent for contract authority. |
| Operational champion | Facilities lead, hotel operations or EHS officer; owns service outcomes. | Offer a concise inspection and a plan they can confidently defend internally. |
| Corporate / economic buyer | Landlord, GM, developer, owner, estate committee or budget holder; weighs spend and risk. | Show the cost of inaction, your specific scope, alternative options and the decision required. |
| Procurement / finance | Confirms documentation, process, vendor registration, PO and payment terms. | Ask for the exact approval sequence and submission deadline. Comply with formal procedures. |
| Final signatory / veto holder | May sit outside the day-to-day team; can approve or block the appointment. | Arrange a joint decision meeting with the operational champion and commercial approver. |

> **The crucial qualification question:** "Suppose the proposal solves the site problem and the budget works: who else must approve it before we can begin, and when would that decision happen?"

## The five-question decision-maker test

1. **Who experiences the problem every day?** Record their name and the concrete service failure they want removed.
2. **Who owns or releases the money?** Identify the budget authority, not just the person who requested a quote.
3. **Who can legally sign or issue a purchase order?** Confirm contract entity, approval limits and committee or tender rules.
4. **Who can block the deal?** Check operations, compliance, finance, procurement and existing service-provider notice periods.
5. **When and how is the choice made?** Record approval meeting date, criteria, documents and next conversation with the actual approver.

## Access ladder: move beyond the gatekeeper

| Step | Action | Example words |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Respect the gatekeeper | Ask about responsibility rather than demanding a phone number. | "Who oversees your waste contract and collection/service performance?" |
| 2 Earn an introduction | Offer the contact something useful to pass forward. | "May I prepare a one-page collection review for your manager?" |
| 3 Request a joint slot | Keep the daily champion in the conversation. | "Would a 15-minute call with you and the person approving suppliers be useful?" |
| 4 Ask for referral permission | Allow the site contact to share or introduce you; use published business channels when appropriate. | "Is it okay to copy you when I send the brief to the manager?" |
| 5 Confirm authority | Before proposal or close, ask who signs, who pays and what papers are required. | "Who will issue the PO if the committee selects us?" |

## Scenario walkthroughs (composite training examples)

### A – The caretaker likes you; the committee has the contract (Westlands apartments)
Current collector often arrives late. The management company appoints suppliers; an owners' committee approves expenditure. Temptation: quote the caretaker and hope it gets forwarded.

> **Rep:** "You know where the collections fail. When this causes resident complaints, who takes responsibility for fixing the contractor?"
> **Caretaker:** "The estate manager gets the complaints, and the committee approves new providers."
> **Rep:** "Would you be comfortable showing the manager a short finding: what causes the delays and how to prevent them?"
> **Caretaker:** "Yes, but the manager is busy."
> **Rep:** "I can make it a one-page brief and a 10-minute discussion. Could you introduce us on WhatsApp or copy us in an email? I will include you, so the operating details are right."
> **Caretaker:** "Send the note first."
> **Rep:** "I will send it this afternoon. Who should it be addressed to, and when is the next committee supplier review?"

- **Value for the caretaker:** a dependable timetable, clear escalation contact(s), fewer residents demanding explanations.
- **Value for manager/committee:** a documented service option and a measurable plan to reduce missed collections.
- **Close on access, not price:** ask for the manager's name, permission to forward the brief, and the committee review date.

CRM: daily contact = caretaker; economic buyer = estate committee; process owner = property manager; intro request = pending; next action = one-page review by 4 p.m.; follow-up = manager discussion within agreed week.

### B – The developer is interested but will not choose the estate collector (Riruta, 350 units near handover)
Site engineer wants an early price; incoming management company will control the operating contract.

> **Rep:** "Who will appoint the domestic-waste provider once the developer hands the property over?"
> **Engineer:** "Probably the management company, after the first residents arrive."
> **Rep:** "Could you introduce me to the person handling service readiness? We can help them estimate bin locations, opening-month volume and a phased collection plan."
> **Engineer:** "The board wants cost savings. Can you help with bins?"
> **Rep:** "We can evaluate a documented bin-support or phased-start option, subject to commercial approval and a clear contract term. I would rather have the management company help design that before we promise anything."
> **Engineer:** "I can arrange a joint walkthrough."
> **Rep:** "Perfect. Can we invite the incoming manager and whoever approves the first-year operations budget?"

- **Legitimate incentive:** a no-obligation readiness assessment; if approved, a transparent company-to-company bin-support arrangement recorded in the contract.
- **Protect the economics:** do not promise free months or bins without modelling initial occupancy, service costs and payback.
- **Respect the procurement handover:** the engineer is an introducer and technical resource, not falsely listed as the contract buyer.

### C – Hotel operations want the service; procurement controls award (hotel in mixed-use mall)
Early-morning cleanliness complaints. Housekeeping wants predictable collections; procurement compares rates; finance wants clear invoicing; the GM can authorise a change if it solves operational risk.

> **Rep:** "If collections are late, whose team has to handle the waste and guest complaints?"
> **Housekeeping:** "Mine. It affects the back-of-house before breakfast."
> **Rep:** "Could we document the actual pickup window and the bags used, then review a scope with you and procurement together?"
> **Procurement:** "I only need the lowest price."
> **Rep:** "I can show a lower-frequency option and a higher-service option on the same basis, including bag supply. Can we confirm which outcome the GM is prepared to fund?"
> **GM:** "Show me what changes on the ground, the first-month risks and how we will verify performance."
> **Rep:** "I will send a one-page service comparison with a sample log, an implementation owner and the decision date. Can we review it on Thursday?"

- Housekeeping: reliable pickup window, clear complaint escalation.
- Procurement & finance: itemised, comparable pricing; predictable exceptions; correct invoicing documentation.
- GM: less hygiene disruption, defined accountability, first-month review.
- **Close:** bring operations and procurement into one decision meeting. A good relationship with housekeeping alone is not a signed contract.

CRM: operational champion = housekeeping head; economic buyer = GM; process = procurement; finance blocker = invoice conditions; proposal recipients = all three as authorised; decision meeting = Thursday.

## Incentive ladder – help the customer, never buy the individual

| What you can offer | Who benefits | Required control |
|---|---|---|
| Free baseline site review or practical layout suggestion | Operations team; manager and procurement gain useful information before choosing. | Keep it bounded; avoid claiming certified audits or guaranteed savings. |
| Written collection plan, escalation matrix or trial measurement report | Manager has fewer complaints and credible evidence to show the board. | Use real metrics and capabilities only; no invented recovery percentages. |
| Two transparently costed service options | Procurement and budget owner select an appropriate scope rather than force a hidden price cut. | Both options must be viable and operations-approved. |
| Approved onboarding, bin-support, volume or prepayment terms | Contracting organisation gains an economic benefit and predictable service. | Cost and approval recorded in a signed quotation/contract; no side deal. |
| Authorised corporate referral or partnership arrangement | An eligible organisation may receive a documented business benefit. | Written policy, disclosure, documented counterparty and management/legal approval. |

> **Hard boundary: no secret personal incentives.** Never offer or pay cash, M-Pesa transfers, gifts, commissions, "appreciation," hiring favours or personal rewards to a customer's employee or decision-maker to secure an award. Do not disguise them as marketing, facilitation or a referral. If asked, decline politely, log the concern factually and escalate. Legitimate commercial incentives belong on the quotation and flow to the contracting organisation under approved terms.

## Turn access into a scheduled decision

### 60-second business case for the approver
1. **Pain:** "Your caretakers receive complaints when collections miss the early gate window."
2. **Cost of waiting:** "An uncollected load interrupts common-area hygiene and occupies management time."
3. **Proposed outcome:** "Fixed collection days, one escalation contact and verifiable service logs."
4. **Two options:** "Base service at feasible frequency; enhanced service with added collections and bags."
5. **Requested decision:** "Approve an operations site walk and a 15-minute scope review by [date]."

### Three-step cadence after an introduction

| When | Outreach | What counts as progress |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | Thank the introducer; send short site findings and the exact decision request. | The decision-maker confirms receipt or delegates a specific reviewer. |
| At agreed follow-up | Call or email to discuss consequences of current service and compare scope options. | Meeting, tender instruction, inspection or written objection – not merely "seen". |
| At decision milestone | Ask for the decision, remaining internal approvals and a realistic next date. | Accept / reject / clear condition; otherwise mark nurture or stalled with reason. |

### CRM record that lets the manager coach the deal

| Required field | Example entry |
|---|---|
| Who uses service? | Caretaker / housekeeper; pain = missed early collections. |
| Economic buyer and signatory | Estate committee approves; management company issues contract; names to confirm. |
| Influencer and introducer | Site engineer agreed to introduce incoming manager on Tuesday. |
| Decision path and date | Ops inspection → committee review → procurement → PO; next review 6 Nov. |
| Customer-facing incentive | Proposed bin support, conditional and not yet approved; company benefit only. |
| Approval status | Ops capacity pending; commercial terms pending; no personal benefit. |
| Next agreed action | Send one-page review by 16:00 and call manager Thursday at 11:00. |

### Metrics that expose "friendly but stuck" deals
Weekly: share of qualified leads with a named economic buyer; introductions requested vs completed; meetings involving decision-makers; proposals reviewed by an approver; time from site visit to decision meeting; concessions approved vs promised; wins activated without service exceptions. **A high visit count with no decision-maker meetings is a warning, not a victory.**

> **One-line field rule:** Never leave a promising site with only "the caretaker liked us." Leave with the person who authorises the change, a reason that person benefits, permission to approach them, and a dated next action.
