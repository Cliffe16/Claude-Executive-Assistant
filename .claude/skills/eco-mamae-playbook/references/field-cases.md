# 04 – Practical field scenarios: ten conversations to rehearse

Anonymised composite scenarios representing realistic Kenyan waste-management sales situations. Dialogue is written for training, **not** reported as an actual client conversation. Each case forces a different choice: timing, scope, logistics, compliance, ethics, finance or recovery.

For role-play use: the **Hidden facts** line is what the buyer reveals only when asked the right question. The **Win condition** is what the seller must achieve.

---

## Case 01 – Timing and occupancy: the apartment that is "almost ready"

**Situation:** A 300-unit residential development in Kilimani is completing finishing works. The project engineer likes your presentation, but the property management company has not taken over. The developer asks for a quotation now.

> **Rep:** "When do residents start moving in, and who will manage waste collection after handover?"
> **Engineer:** "We hope next month. Management has not been formally appointed."
> **Rep:** "Will you need construction-waste removal before handover, or regular domestic waste after occupancy?"
> **Engineer:** "Both, but not at the same time."
> **Rep:** "I'll separate those scopes. May I meet the incoming manager before finalising the residential schedule?"

- **Make two opportunities, not one:** a time-bound construction removal project now and a recurring residential contract linked to occupancy.
- **Offer a mobilisation milestone:** reassess projected bags per day when occupancy passes an agreed threshold.
- **Avoid artificial certainty:** units built are not units occupied; do not forecast full recurring revenue on day one.

**CRM note:** Opportunity A: one-off site clearance, engineering buyer, survey pending. Opportunity B: monthly estate waste service, decision-maker not yet assigned, next follow-up one week before expected handover.

**Hidden facts (drill "Apartment handover"):** full occupancy will take 8 months; the engineer cannot approve an ongoing supplier.
**Win condition:** seller secures an introduction to incoming management and separates the service scopes.

## Case 02 – Scope trade-off: the hotel wants six collections; the budget buys three

**Situation:** A hotel at a busy retail complex wants collections every morning, 500+ large bags a month and a report showing where waste goes. Procurement says its ceiling is below the cost of the proposed service.

> **Buyer:** "Can you match this budget and still collect six mornings?"
> **Rep:** "I can't responsibly guarantee six without confirming the route and cost. Which service elements are essential?"
> **Buyer:** "Guest areas must be clean before breakfast. The bag supply matters too."
> **Rep:** "Let me compare a three-day schedule with larger bin capacity against an enhanced six-day plan. We will show bag supply and reporting separately."
> **Buyer:** "Fine – but reporting must be credible."
> **Rep:** "We will only list the records our operations team can actually produce, then show a sample format."

- **Separate specification from budget:** price collection frequency, bags, bin servicing and evidence separately.
- **Test operational reality:** daily collection windows, loading-bay restrictions, vehicle route and weekend staffing.
- **Close on a choice:** ask which scope the hotel prefers after seeing the cost/service trade-off.

**CRM note:** Stage = Proposal preparation; buyer = procurement + hotel operations; mandatory early window; two-option quote awaiting ops approval; decision meeting Friday.

**Hidden facts (drill "Hotel budget"):** breakfast service is non-negotiable; bag supply can be purchased separately.
**Win condition:** seller offers genuine service options without misrepresenting daily collection.

## Case 03 – Route and service design: the CBD building where access is the real problem

**Situation:** A mixed-use building on a narrow CBD street has small waste volumes, but security only allows truck access between 05:30 and 06:15. The caretaker wants "cheap collection".

> **Caretaker:** "Other collectors say they can do it for much less."
> **Rep:** "What happens if a truck arrives after the gate closes?"
> **Caretaker:** "The waste stays until the next day. Then tenants complain."
> **Rep:** "So the critical deliverable is a reliable early access slot, not just the number of bags?"
> **Caretaker:** "Exactly. Our manager wants no complaints."
> **Rep:** "I'll ask operations to confirm the route time and give you a schedule we can stand behind."

- **Reframe the comparison:** the client's economic problem is missed access and complaints, not just collection price.
- **Quote only confirmed windows:** if our existing route cannot serve it, revise the time or decline.
- **Define the handover point:** who puts bags out, unlocks the gate and signs the collection log?

**CRM note:** Site = CBD, access 05:30–06:15, frequency to confirm, buyer = facility manager, ops route sign-off required before proposal.

## Case 04 – Hazard identification: "These are just empty chemical drums"

**Situation:** An industrial prospect in Athi River asks for urgent removal of drums previously used for a corrosive chemical. The site supervisor calls them empty and requests the same charge as routine waste.

> **Supervisor:** "They're empty. Can you pick them tomorrow with your normal truck?"
> **Rep:** "What was stored in them, and has residue status been documented?"
> **Supervisor:** "Hydrochloric acid, but there's hardly anything left."
> **Rep:** "That changes the assessment. I cannot book this as general waste. I'll request the safety and classification details and refer the job for compliance review."
> **Supervisor:** "Can you at least give me a price now?"
> **Rep:** "After the qualified team confirms the handling, transport and receiving facility, we can quote the actual compliant scope."

- **Stop the normal quote:** previous hazardous contents require proper classification and a specialist operational review.
- **Request documentation:** SDS, quantity, packaging condition, residue information and permitted handling route.
- **Escalate:** only authorised personnel approve the waste category, vehicle, transport, receiving facility and paperwork.

**CRM note:** Stage = Compliance review; waste = chemical drums, content history HCl, quantity and documentation pending; do not schedule pickup.

**Hidden facts (drill "Chemical drums"):** containers held acid; there are no safety documents ready.
**Win condition:** seller refuses normal collection, gathers facts and escalates.

## Case 05 – Hidden cost and pricing: the construction job that looks profitable – until loading

**Situation:** A site manager requests a "full truck" removal of mixed rubble and timber. A competitor offers a flat fee. The truck cannot reverse into the loading area, and the job may require a second trip.

> **Manager:** "Just tell me the cost of a truckload."
> **Rep:** "I'll give a useful figure once we confirm what fills the truck. Is it rubble, timber, soil or a mix?"
> **Manager:** "Mostly rubble, but workers will load it."
> **Rep:** "Is the loading point accessible and is material ready on arrival? What happens if it exceeds one safe vehicle load?"
> **Manager:** "We may need more than one trip."
> **Rep:** "Then the quote should state the per-trip load specification, permitted material and variation approval before extra trips."

- **Define units:** trip price, safe capacity, loading responsibility, loading wait time and excluded material.
- **Validate disposal:** approved receiving facility and expected handling/tipping costs.
- **Control variations:** extra trips, delays or changed material require written acceptance before work.

**CRM note:** Stage = Site inspection completed; volume uncertain; one or two trips possible; next action = survey and scoped per-trip offer.

## Case 06 – Price objection: "Your competitor is 25% cheaper"

**Situation:** An estate manager says the incumbent has quoted materially less. You suspect the competitor collects less frequently but have no proof.

> **Manager:** "Why should I pay more for waste collection?"
> **Rep:** "That is a fair question. May we compare the scope line by line? How many collections, bags, response commitments and reports are included?"
> **Manager:** "Their price includes two pickups, no bags."
> **Rep:** "Our proposed package includes three pickups and bag supply. I can cost a two-day option for a fair comparison, subject to a site check."
> **Manager:** "Let me see both."
> **Rep:** "I'll show the differences clearly so your committee can decide what it actually needs."

- **Never attack the incumbent:** use a like-for-like service comparison rather than unsupported claims.
- **Keep a defendable floor:** do not discount below the approved economics.
- **Trade, do not concede:** if price falls, scope may need to change transparently.

**CRM note:** Stage = Negotiation; key objection = competitor cheaper; action = two-scope comparison with same waste assumptions; committee review Wednesday.

**Hidden facts (drill "Cheap competitor"):** competitor quote excludes bags and one weekly visit.
**Win condition:** seller asks for a like-for-like comparison and avoids unsupported claims.

## Case 07 – Ethics and escalation: procurement asks for a "personal appreciation"

**Situation:** A manager says the contract can be awarded quickly if you pay a personal commission not described in the tender or company procurement policy.

> **Manager:** "There is a small appreciation for me and then we can sign."
> **Rep:** "We can only work through official, documented procurement and payment processes."
> **Manager:** "Everyone does it. It's the only way."
> **Rep:** "I understand your position, but we cannot make personal payments for an award. If there is an approved supplier fee, please share the official requirement with our office."
> **Manager:** "Then I cannot help."
> **Rep:** "Thank you for clarifying. I'll record the position and step away from this process."

- **Do not negotiate the amount:** decline and document the concern.
- **Inform leadership:** escalate promptly through appropriate internal channels.
- **Protect the team:** do not route the payment through an agent or disguise it as a discount.

**CRM note:** Stage = Disqualified / ethics concern; neutral factual note, limited access; no personal allegations in customer-facing messages.

## Case 08 – Cash-flow risk: the deal is good, but the customer pays in 60 days

**Situation:** A factory offers attractive recurring volume but says all suppliers are paid 60 days after invoice. Collections would require fuel, workers and disposal expenditure from week one.

> **Finance:** "We pay after sixty days. That is standard here."
> **Rep:** "Thank you. For recurring service, that changes the working-capital requirement. Could you consider a mobilisation deposit or a shorter first-month cycle?"
> **Finance:** "Maybe, if it is in the contract."
> **Rep:** "I'll take the proposed terms for commercial approval and return with a structure that is clear to both teams."
> **Finance:** "Send the options."
> **Rep:** "I'll also confirm the exact invoice documents finance requires so payment does not stall later."

- **Separate revenue from cash:** a high monthly fee can still be an unsuitable contract if payment timing is unfinanceable.
- **Offer structured alternatives:** deposit, fortnightly billing or a documented first-month payment milestone, if approved.
- **Do not self-authorise credit:** finance/commercial lead must approve exceptions and client onboarding.

**CRM note:** Stage = Commercial negotiation; payment risk = 60-day credit; next action = request first-month deposit / term approval by Thursday.

## Case 09 – Capacity honesty: the customer wants a start date operations cannot confirm

**Situation:** A prospect on an outer route asks for six collections per week. Current truck capacity is already committed, and the hired truck has restricted availability. The buyer wants a start tomorrow.

> **Buyer:** "Can you begin tomorrow? We have no collector."
> **Rep:** "I can move urgently, but I need a confirmed dispatch slot before promising the start."
> **Buyer:** "Can you just say yes and sort out the route?"
> **Rep:** "I'd rather give you a start date we can deliver. I'll check with operations now and call back by the agreed time with confirmed options."
> **Buyer:** "What if six days is impossible?"
> **Rep:** "Then we can discuss a phased schedule, extra capacity if approved, or decline rather than let waste accumulate."

- **Escalate immediately:** check route density, vehicle availability, drivers, loaders and disposal window.
- **Use a conditional close:** "Subject to dispatch confirmation" is not the same as a confirmed agreement.
- **Offer capacity choices:** phased rollout, scheduled fixed days or approved subcontracting with proper oversight.

**CRM note:** Stage = Ops feasibility hold; required = six weekly collections; owner = sales rep; next action = dispatch confirmation before any start promise.

## Case 10 – Service recovery and retention: after the first missed collection, the client threatens to leave

**Situation:** A new client sends a photo of an overflowing waste area after a collection was missed. The property manager says the committee will cancel unless someone responds immediately.

> **Client:** "You promised reliability. This is unacceptable."
> **Rep:** "You are right to expect the agreed service. I'm sorry the pickup was missed. I'm contacting dispatch now and will give you a verified recovery plan by 10:30."
> **Client:** "I don't want excuses."
> **Rep:** "Understood. I will confirm the corrected pickup time, the person responsible and what we are changing to avoid recurrence."
> **Client:** "Send it in writing."
> **Rep:** "I will. I'll also check with you after the collection has been completed."

- **Own the issue without guessing:** acknowledge, verify facts with dispatch and give a specific update time.
- **Close the loop with evidence:** confirm actual pickup, communicate root cause after review, document corrective action.
- **Protect the relationship:** schedule a short seven-day service check; avoid blaming drivers in public.

**CRM note:** Client issue linked to account; missed collection date, cause pending investigation, recovery owner and promised update time logged; escalation to operations.

**Hidden facts (drill "Overdue first pickup"):** client is angry but will stay if recovery is credible and timely.
**Win condition:** seller owns the incident, verifies with operations and closes the loop.
