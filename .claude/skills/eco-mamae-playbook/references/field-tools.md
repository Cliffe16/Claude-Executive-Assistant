# 09 – Field tools: messages, checklists, practice

## Ready-to-use messages

The playbook lists four message templates – **Cold call: 45-second structure**, **Decision-maker introduction request**, **After-visit WhatsApp**, **Proposal email** – but their text did not survive PDF extraction. **Confirm the approved wording with Sales leadership.** Until then, `/followup` drafts these from the playbook's principles:

- **Cold call (45 s):** who you are → one observation about their site → the problem we solve (missed collections, complaints, management time) → ask who oversees the waste contract → request a short discussion.
- **Decision-maker introduction request:** offer something useful to pass forward (one-page collection review), keep the site contact included, ask permission to copy them.
- **After-visit WhatsApp:** thanks, the problem in one sentence, missing information, what you will send and by when, the agreed next step.
- **Proposal email:** the two options, what changes on the ground, approval conditions, decision date, who should attend the review.

## One-minute site audit checklist – record before you leave

- [ ] Economic buyer, signatory, influencer, introduction permission, existing provider and notice period
- [ ] Waste types, estimated volume and peak days; occupancy stage
- [ ] Pickup point, route, time restrictions and access/security
- [ ] Required bags, bins, special handling and reporting
- [ ] Start date, billing entity, payment term and approval risk
- [ ] Customer-facing incentive and approval status; next action, owner, date and buyer confirmation

## Practice and coach for judgment

One rep plays seller, one buyer, one observer. Give the seller only the initial context; the buyer reveals hidden facts **only when asked**. After each round, repeat the conversation with a different tactic.

### Role-play drills (10 minutes each)

The PDF heading says "five drills" but the table lists six.

| Drill | Hidden buyer fact | Win condition |
|---|---|---|
| Apartment handover | Full occupancy will take 8 months and the engineer cannot approve an ongoing supplier. | Seller secures an introduction to incoming management and separates service scopes. |
| Caretaker gatekeeper | Caretaker likes the pitch but only the management company and committee may appoint suppliers. | Seller earns a permission-based introduction to the actual approver. |
| Hotel budget | Breakfast service is non-negotiable; bag supply can be purchased separately. | Seller offers genuine service options without misrepresenting daily collection. |
| Chemical drums | Containers held acid; there are no safety documents ready. | Seller refuses normal collection, gathers facts and escalates. |
| Cheap competitor | Competitor quote excludes bags and one weekly visit. | Seller asks for like-for-like comparison and avoids unsupported claims. |
| Overdue first pickup | Client is angry but will stay if recovery is credible and timely. | Seller owns incident, verifies operations and closes the loop. |

### Role-play scoring – 20 points (5 capabilities × 0–4)

| Capability | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem diagnosis | None | Generic | One issue | Impact probed | Impact quantified |
| Decision mapping | None | Assumes | Contact named | Approver known | Process & date known |
| Operational judgment | Unsafe | Guess | Some checks | Feasibility clear | Escalation precise |
| Value communication | Price only | Generic pitch | Partial fit | Clear outcome | Evidence-led fit |
| Next-step control | None | Vague | Action | Action + owner | Action + owner + date |

**Scoring guide:** 0–9 = repeat with coaching; 10–15 = ready for supervised calls; 16–20 = ready for independent discovery, subject to company approval rules.
**A serious compliance or ethics error means the exercise is unsuccessful, irrespective of points.**

### Manager's weekly coaching prompts

1. Which opportunity should we stop chasing? Why?
2. Show me one CRM record where a next action drove movement.
3. Which deal could hurt service reliability if won tomorrow?
4. Where did you protect the price by changing scope rather than discounting?
5. Which active account or client experience could become a genuine referral after permission?
